AIR CARGO SPACE RECOVERY

  
Every week, cargo space is booked, then flies empty.

  
Forwarders cancel late, send less than they booked, or never show up. SpaceBack gives your capacity team one place to see what changed, reads forwarder replies, and helps them resell freed space before cut-off, with a person approving every step.

  
 No login or setup. Made-up data.

  


  
The problem

  

    
15–30%
of cargo bookings are no-shows or cancellations in the last three days before departure, as a network average.
AGIFORS abstract, "Cargo Show-Up Rate Prediction" (Sabre), describing work developed with the Etihad Cargo team

    
Fees don't fill the hold
Some airlines charge for late changes and no-shows, for example up to 100% on certain large Qantas Freight shipments and a 50% no-show fee at ITA Airways Cargo on shipments over 300 kg. A fee recovers some money, but the space can still fly empty.
Published conditions: Qantas Freight, ITA Airways Cargo

    
Prediction is not recovery
Airlines and vendors can already predict which bookings may not show up. Our hypothesis, still to be tested, is that the follow-through is the gap: asking the forwarder, reading the reply, releasing the space and reselling it in time.
Example of prediction and capacity tools: Wiremind

How SpaceBack closes the gap

  

    
1 · CheckEvery flight is checked automatically: what was cancelled, what is free, what is at risk and why, and what could fill it. Requests are prepared at 72 hours.

    
2 · AskA person approves reconfirmation requests to at-risk forwarders.

    
3 · ReadThe agent reads each reply ("only 2 of 4 pallets") and proposes keep, reduce, release, chase or escalate.

    
4 · ApproveA person approves every release. Fees come from the fee table, not the AI.

    
5 · ResellFreed space is matched to waiting requests whose cargo can reach the terminal by cut-off. A person approves each offer.

  

  
Green outline: a person decides.


  
What runs by itself, and what a person decides

  

    

      
StepWhoIn a real airline

      
        
Update space and riskAutomaticPlain rules, re-run whenever the booking system reports a change. Fast and cheap; needs a connection to that system, or a regular import until one exists.

        
Read forwarder repliesAutomaticThe AI runs only when a reply arrives, which keeps its cost low. Needs access to the shared mailbox.

        
Prepare requests and proposalsAutomaticReady and waiting in a queue, so nobody has to start a check.

        
Send, release, offerA personThese affect customers and money, so a person approves each one. Routine approvals could be loosened only after a pilot shows they are safe.

Are we solving a real problem?

  
The problem looks real. We have not yet proven that this solution solves it: no airline has tested it, and this prototype runs on made-up data.

  

    

      
PartSolves a problem?Honest reason

      
        
The problem itselfLikely yesThe 15–30% figure comes from airline industry work, but it is a network average and the size of the loss will differ by airline.

        
Departure boardPartlyIt makes the work easier to see. Airlines probably already have capacity screens, so it is the way in, not the value.

        
Alert on changesPartlyIt helps only if controllers find out too late today, which we have not confirmed.

        
Asking forwardersPartlyThe first message is a template. The value is in doing it consistently and on time.

        
Reading repliesYes, if manual todayThis is the main gap we target: vague free-text replies turned into a proposal a person can approve.

        
Revenue impact viewPartlyIt helps a controller prioritise and helps an airline judge the value, but it is an estimate and earns nothing by itself.

        
Approval by a personNot directlyIt makes the product safe and trustworthy, but saves little time by itself.

        
Reselling spaceYes, when a buyer fitsThis is where money is recovered, but it needs waiting demand and enough time before cut-off.

      
    

  

  

    
What a pilot with an airline would need to prove

    
Do current tools already do this reconfirmation well? How much of the lost space can really be resold in time? Would a capacity controller use it every day? We make no claim about recovered revenue until those are answered.

  


  
Safety, security and compliance

  
SpaceBack supports commercial capacity decisions. It does not replace load control, dangerous goods acceptance or security checks. We make no compliance claim for this prototype; a real deployment needs review by the airline's own teams and regulators.

  

    

      
AreaIn this prototypeBefore any real use

      
        
Dangerous goodsNever accepts them. Any change to the cargo goes to the acceptance team, with the fictional rules pre-checked.Align with IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations and the airline's approved acceptance process.

        
Load and balanceNot checked. Totals of weight and volume only.Load control stays the authority. Feed in pallet sizes, positions and weight limits.

        
Cargo securityNot checked. An offer does not confirm a shipment's security status.Keep the airline's secure supply chain checks (for example, regulated agent status and screening) in the offer flow.

        
Customs and documentsAir waybill status only.Check advance cargo information and customs deadlines for each route.

        
Data protectionMade-up data only.Review where forwarder messages go, data-transfer rules and retention. Run the AI through the airline's approved, server-side setup.

        
AI oversightA person approves every action. Code handles fees and rule checks. Stress tests are published in the Quality checks tab.Keep a permanent audit trail, security testing, monitoring and a way to fall back to manual work.

      
    

  


  
Where this goes next

  

    
THIS PROTOTYPE
Prove the workflow

      
  • Departure dashboard, risk reasons and Space Ledger
  • AI reads replies; a person approves every action
  • Freed space offered to waiting requests after rule checks
  • Rough revenue estimate

    
NEXT SCOPE
Make it plannable: a pilot on one route

      
  • As a controller, I can move a booked shipment to another flight, with the forwarder's agreement.
  • As a load planner, I see pallet sizes, positions and weight limits before an offer goes out.
  • As a capacity manager, I see chargeable weight, allotments, connections and priority cargo.
  • As an airline, I connect a read-only booking feed and shared mailbox, and keep a permanent audit trail.

    
THE SCOPE AFTER THAT
Make it network-wide

      
  • As a network controller, I rebalance space across stations, freighters and passenger belly hold.
  • As a revenue manager, I use the airline's own show-up predictions to decide who to ask first.
  • As a forwarder, I confirm, reduce or accept an offer myself in a portal.
  • As an airline, I can allow low-risk approvals to run automatically, only after the evidence supports it.

  


  
What it will not do

  
Release space a forwarder has confirmed, act on a prediction alone, waive or invent fees, accept dangerous goods, or check pallet positions and aircraft balance. "Can be filled" is an estimate until a load planner confirms it. This is a prototype with a made-up airline, flights and forwarders, not connected to any real system.

How to read these numbers

    
Booked weight × each booking's rate, plus fees from the fee table. Expected now assumes every remaining booking flies; at-risk revenue is shown separately. This is revenue, not profit: it leaves out handling costs and fee waivers, and an offer earns nothing unless the forwarder accepts. All figures come from made-up data.
