# Falcon Cargo Air: Space Recovery Policy (fictional)

Simplified, fictional rules for a prototype. Cite them by section, for example "Space 3.2".

## 1. When the space check runs

1.1 A controller can open a flight's space check at any time. The first alert and the reconfirmation requests go out 72 hours before departure (D-3). Cancellations and reductions received at any time are handled the same way, until the acceptance cut-off.
1.2 Only bookings marked at risk (section 2) are contacted. Other bookings are left alone.
1.3 The first reconfirmation request uses the airline's approved template. Follow-up messages are drafted case by case.
1.4 All times are UTC.

## 2. Risk signals (worked out by the app, not by the agent)

2.1 Signal R1: the forwarder's show-up rate over the last 90 days is below 0.80.
2.2 Signal R2: no air waybill has been issued by D-3.
2.3 Signal R3: the booking was made more than 14 days before departure on a flight in a peak period.
2.4 Signal R4: the booking may duplicate one the forwarder holds on another flight or carrier.
2.5 A booking is at risk when it has two or more of R1, R2 and R3, or R4 on its own.
2.6 Risk signals are reasons to ask the forwarder. They are never reasons to release space.

## 3. Releasing space

3.1 Space is released only when the forwarder says in writing that the shipment is cancelled or smaller.
3.2 Never release space the forwarder has confirmed, and never release space on risk signals, history or predictions alone, whoever asks for it.
3.3 Release exactly what the forwarder gave up. Use the forwarder's own figure (for example "about 1,200 kg"). If the forwarder gives pieces but no volume, reduce the volume in proportion to the pieces. Never release more than the forwarder gave up.
3.4 If there is no reply after the request and two chasers, do not release anything. Escalate to the controller by 24 hours before departure.
3.5 If the reply is vague ("probably", "will know tomorrow"), chase again with one clear question and a deadline of 12:00 UTC the day before departure.
3.6 A release above 2,000 kg needs a senior capacity manager. Escalate it with the proposal attached.

## 4. Messages to forwarders

4.1 Keep messages short and polite, use exact numbers, and make one clear request.
4.2 Never tell a forwarder their space has been released, or state a fee amount. Fees and releases are confirmed by a person.
4.3 Never threaten a forwarder or argue with them.

## 5. Worked examples

5.1 "We will ship 3 of the 5 pieces, about 900 kg" on a 1,500 kg, 5-piece, 9.0 m3 booking: Reduce, release 600 kg and 3.6 m3.
5.2 "Please cancel, the client changed plans": Release all.
5.3 "Let me check with the shipper": Chase again, asking for a yes or no and the weight by the deadline in 3.5.
